## 1. The goal as I read it

The maintainer wants one thing from Horde and states it once, clearly: *regardless of codebase size, work on it effectively*. His mechanism is context: a human team is cut per area so that what each member must hold is bounded, and an agent that is one context for a whole repository "has to jump through files, grep, look for everything in different places, and its context is impossible to fit." So the horde should self-organise on the graph Yggdrasil already declares, at whatever level of the hierarchy makes a bounded context (a whole module, or just the front-end), with an architect drawing in thick lines and consulting owners instead of reading code, owners deciding the inside of their area and raising law there, the why-log as the only knowledge outside code and aspects, and refinement distributed so that "the one main agent does not do it blindly." The user is the client: orders the outcome, has the final word on what and how at the top, and does not run the process.

Where I think his framing is wrong, plainly. First, the goal is about *context*, and the design he built is about *authority*: eight roles, keys, approvals, leases, a veto, dissent, an auditor. Those are what a human organisation needs because humans forget, tire, and have interests; the family's own thesis (`VISION.md`, "Who does what") says the law coordinates and the machine proves everything between the two human ends. Every seat that exists to *review* is a seat that exists because the law is incomplete, and the maintainer's own rule for that case is "if there is a pattern, it is enforced by an aspect; the fewer implicit things the better, and best none at all." Taken seriously, that rule dissolves most of the org chart into legislation plus a gate. Second, "Superpowers is strongly linear" is half true: its plan already carries per-task interfaces (`writing-plans/SKILL.md:92-94`) and it ships a parallel-dispatch skill (`dispatching-parallel-agents/SKILL.md:14,77`); what is linear is one sentence in its executor, "Never dispatch multiple implementation subagents in parallel" (`subagent-driven-development/SKILL.md:282`), and the hard approval gate before any code (`brainstorming/SKILL.md:14-20`). The thing to replace was that sentence and that gate, not the shape. Third, he said he built Horde "to optimise for the presence of Yggdrasil," and Horde 0.4 does optimise on Yggdrasil's *graph* (nodes as the unit of work) while duplicating Yggdrasil's *proof* (a verifier key, owner approvals, an auditor) instead of resting on it. The optimisation landed on the wrong half.

## 2. Responsibility boundaries

What each member must own for the goal to hold, stated so that nothing is owned twice.

**Yggdrasil owns the cut, the law and the proof.** The cut: nodes with mappings, relations, ports, types (`docs/nodes.md`), and a file has exactly one owner or none (`docs/nodes.md:60`). The law: aspects with a status ladder, `when` predicates, `implies`, ports carrying aspects. The proof: every (aspect, unit) pair verified deterministically where it can be and by a separate model call where it must be ("one LLM verifying the work of another", `docs/reviewers.md:28`), recorded in a lock CI re-proves without a key (`docs/the-lock.md`). It also owns what the loop needs to *know* about the code, as machine documents: who owns a file and which rules reach it (`yg context --json`, `docs/cli-reference.md:78-136`), who depends on a node (`yg impact --json`), the whole verification state (`yg check --json`), every rule with its status and last log line (`yg aspects --json`, `docs/cli-reference.md:818-828`), a rule's own history (`yg aspects log`), the attention feed and the human-signed decisions on it (`yg advise`, `docs/cli-reference.md:859-921`), and the incident ledger as human testimony only (`docs/cli-reference.md:964-1006`). Two things it owns that Horde must not reimplement: the log gate that forces a why before verification (`docs/the-lock.md:90-104`, `docs/nodes.md:105`) and the rule that lowering is a human's (`docs/reviewers.md:657` on suppressions, `docs/aspect-status.md:143-144` on downgrades, `docs/cli-reference.md:915` on dismissals, `AGENTS.md:135-141`).

**Grain owns evidence about practice, never a verdict.** The first graph for a repository that has none (`README.md:23-51`), what a place expects and which exemplar to copy (`README.md:235-245`), what a new file must come with, what co-changes, and, for a graph that exists, where a finer cut beats on its own evidence (`README.md:243`). It "does not decide what a codebase must never do" (`README.md:81`) and "informs and never blocks" (`README.md:346`). In the loop it is the worker's map and the source of quality tickets, nothing more.

**Horde must own exactly what the two below cannot know: the mission.** Refinement of a client's sentence onto the graph; the queue as a DAG derived from what the tickets declare (`queue.mjs:893-1096` already does this); one tree per writer; the diff-level checks Yggdrasil has no notion of because it judges trees, not changes: a new test fails on the base (`premerge.mjs:447-501`), the diff stays in its declared files (`premerge.mjs:372-392`), a mapping lands with the first file (`premerge.mjs:672-686`), no mission language in graph text (`premerge.mjs:627-658`); cost per landed ticket; the plain-language rendering of what the mission did to the law; and the loop that ticks. Horde must not own: a second store of knowledge about a node (the node charter, `node.mjs:1634-1649`, is one), a second judge of prose rules beside Yggdrasil's, or a second notion of who is alive.

**Ratatoskr owns the client-facing register at both ends** (never name an artifact, product decisions only, `SKILL.md:20,329`). **Urd owns the worker's stop:** when the spec runs out, ask rather than guess, at the point of doubt (`SKILL.md:18,24,71`). In an autonomous loop "ask" means write the question where the tick will find it and stop. **Researcher owns measurement**, including the audit, because an audit is a sample with an interval, which is an experiment, not a seat (`SKILL.md:88,167,219`).

**The client owns** intent, product decisions, the final yes on rendered results (`VISION.md`, "The human stands at both ends"), and every lowering of law.

**What none of them owns today.** (1) The loop: today a human keeps it alive by waking stewards (`SKILL.md:158-160`; H6, H13). (2) A signal that the law is incomplete: the audit measures the verifier's refutation rate (`wave.mjs:629-638`), nothing measures how many review comments should have been rules. (3) Non-test evidence (a film, a screenshot) is "reproduced" by an agent looking at it (`verifier.md:51-52`); nobody in the family judges an artifact that is not text. (4) Reviewer cost per ticket: `cost.mjs` counts reviewer calls per mission only ("Only whole-mission: an event names a pair, not a ticket", `cost.mjs:61,99`). (5) The production signal (gap 4), outside 6.0.0, as the index says. (6) The law in the client's words: parts exist, nothing renders them.

## 3. Shapes compared

**A. Horde 0.4 with D1–D3 applied.** Eight roles on Agent Teams: the steward and the architect are long-lived teammates the session raises, owners/workers/verifiers are the steward's subagents, the auditor and counsel are the session's (`model.md:49-63`, `topology.md:5-32`; the platform constraint in Horde's own `CLAUDE.md:13`). Coordination by doorbells and briefs; liveness by timestamps although the doc says "by files, never by silence" (`topology.md:197` against `roster.mjs:521-528,546-550,589-592`). Merges need two keys and per-node approvals bound to a patch-id (`tk.mjs:862-945`, `premerge.mjs:228-299`), an auditor resamples merged tickets (`wave.mjs:629-638`). The law climbs by evidence (`node.mjs:835-997`), only the architect proposes and files, and the filing is a hand edit the tool prints ("file it into the graph yourself", `node.mjs:1735,1891`; `escalate.mjs:327`).

**B. The previous review's model.** Keeps the director, a steward and an architect as teammates, one-shot workers, verifiers, auditor and counsel; splits the owner into a planner and a reviewer per *territory* (a lease-sized set of whole nodes); one queue with `t-`/`g-` items; desks that a `horde tick` re-rings; approvals computed once at `landed` with a wide-threshold architect key; a judge policy at landing; a gate runner with a lock; the contract mirror per horde.

**C. The thin shape.** Superpowers plus Yggdrasil: brainstorm, a plan as a task list with interfaces, one fresh subagent per task with a task review and a final review (`subagent-driven-development/SKILL.md:12`), `yg check` as the only gate, no standing roles.

**D. No separate Horde.** The loop's needs added to `yg`.

**E. The recommendation (section 4).** No standing agents. The mission is refined onto territories by one-shots; the queue is a DAG in files; every ticket is one ephemeral worker in one worktree; landing is a mechanical gate that includes Yggdrasil's own reviewer; law is raised by the agents that work a territory and lowered only by the client; a `tick` script drives everything and a teammate, if present, runs the tick instead of the session.

## 4. The recommendation

One sentence: **Horde is the mission layer of the law — a refinement that cuts a client's sentence into territories on Yggdrasil's graph and a tick that runs one ephemeral worker per ticket in one worktree, lands each through a mechanical gate whose judge is Yggdrasil's own reviewer, lets the agents of a territory raise the law and only the client lower it, and reads every result back to the client in plain words.**

### 4.1 Roles, and why each survives "the gate would have caught it anyway"

The test is the family's: a standing role earns its place only if the gate could not have caught what it catches. Applied to A's eight:

- **Steward.** Dispatch, reconcile, merge, liveness. Every action is "read files, decide by rule, write files" (`steward.md:111-205` is a procedure, and the brief says the steward "holds no opinion the charter does not give"). That is a script. *Becomes `horde tick`.* Cost of keeping it as an agent: a Sonnet context re-reading the queue every turn and a wake-up problem.
- **Verifier.** Reproduces evidence and runs the revert test and the gate (`verifier.md:48-77`): all mechanical, and `premerge` already re-runs the revert test and the gate itself (`premerge.mjs:447-556`). Its one judgment is prose rules under its own name via `yg verdict` (`verifier.md:78-86`, `node.mjs:403-415`), which is Yggdrasil's reviewer wearing a badge. *Dissolves into the landing gate plus Yggdrasil's reviewer tier, or a judge one-shot bound to pending pairs when the repository has no key.* "Never verifies its own work" is preserved where it always was: the reviewer is a separate call from the coder (`docs/reviewers.md:28`).
- **Owner.** Three functions in one seat: hold the node's context, review every change in it, propose tickets and law. The context is in files by the model's own rule ("Nothing lives in an agent's head", `model.md:96`), so a fresh spawn reads what a live owner would have remembered. The review either enforces a pattern, in which case the maintainer's rule makes it an aspect and the gate catches the next one, or it is taste, which goes to the log (`review.md:24-26`). *The owner becomes three one-shot functions over a territory: consult (at refinement), work (the ticket), legislate (after a wave or on a worker's flag), and its approval key is deleted.* This is Ruling 2 taken further than the session took it: the owner legislates, it does not sign.
- **Architect.** Rules on graph changes and reviews the plan. The gate cannot say whether a boundary is coherent; that is design. *Survives as a one-shot at refinement (cut the territories, review the derived plan) and per graph-change item during the mission.* Its key on graph items is the one human-style key that survives, because a rule cannot judge a rule's placement. `yg-architecture.yaml` and suppressions stay the client's, as today (`architect.md:36-40`).
- **Auditor.** Redoes a merged ticket in full to measure the verifier (`auditor.md:14-28`). With no verifier and a mechanical gate, what is left to sample is the *reviewer's* judgment, and Yggdrasil already carries the instruments: `yg aspect-test --repeat` for self-consistency (`docs/reviewers.md:591`), the drill corpus with `yg drill add` for escapes (`docs/reviewers.md:579-585`), and the model-swap protocol. *Becomes a Researcher measurement the tick can run at a configured rate (4.8), never a gate.*
- **Counsel.** A one-shot opinion. It is just a subagent the session spawns; no state, no role definition needed. *Deleted as a role, kept as a habit.*
- **Director.** The user's session. *Stays as the session: it talks to the client under Ratatoskr, runs refinement, runs the tick when no teammate does, and answers build questions itself.* It rules on nothing that a file cannot rule on.
- **Worker.** *Stays*, as the only agent that writes code, one per ticket, one worktree, cheapest class that passes.

### 4.2 The lifecycle of one assignment

1. **The sentence.** The client says what should be true. The session reads it back in product words (Ratatoskr's read-back shape) and records the charter: goal, non-goals, the evidence catalogue with row ids (kept from 0.4, `templates/charter.md:19-28`).
2. **Refinement** (4.3): `horde refine` runs the architect one-shot to cut territories, then one consult one-shot per territory in parallel, then the architect again over the derived plan. The output is a queue of tickets with files, produces, consumes, evidence rows, class, and a list of law each consult wants to raise. The session renders the frame to the client: what will change where, what proves it, what the law will gain. The client says go. Nothing has been dispatched.
3. **The tick** (4.7): every N minutes, or on every session turn: reconcile running branches, land the landed ones, dispatch the ready ones as parallel subagents, close a wave when the queue drains.
4. **The worker**: one ticket, one worktree cut from the trunk tip, the territory's context (rules per file from `yg context --file`, node descriptions and logs, `grain where`/`obligation`/`how`), the ticket body, the discipline texts for TDD and debugging (kept from `reference/discipline/`). It commits with the repository's own hooks, logs its why with `yg log add` where the type requires it, and its last act is `horde land <t>`, which refuses a dirty tree outside the declared files and records the landed sha. If the spec runs out, it writes the question to the ticket log and stops (Urd); the tick surfaces it.
5. **Landing** (mechanical, in a scratch detached worktree at the branch tip): base freshness; scope; proof (revert test, or the mutation variant of H14, or `no-new-tests` judged on evidence rows); the repository's gate under a per-repository lock (H26); `yg check --approve --only-deterministic`, then the prose pairs the change reached filled by Yggdrasil's reviewer tier or by a judge one-shot through `yg verdict`; a full `yg check` green; every added file owned by a node; graph text; the law guard (4.5). Green: merge `--no-ff` into trunk, delete the worktree and branch, journal. Red: the ticket goes to `changes` with the gate's own words, the fix-loop breaker counts the round (`tk.mjs:290-327`, kept), a fresh worker takes over after the resume rounds with the log.
6. **Wave close**: evidence rows turned green, the quality index and its delta (`node.mjs:451-501`, kept), the ladder's observations (`node.mjs:1105-1134`, kept), the law diff rendered for the client (4.6), cost, and quality tickets refreshed from `grain advise` filtered to leased territories (`queue.mjs:412-503`, kept with its filter).
7. **Done**: every evidence row reproduced, the trunk gate green at the tip, cost on file (`horde.mjs:699-803`, minus the audit condition). The session presents the branch; the push is the client's.

### 4.3 Where refinement happens and who is consulted

Refinement is the one phase that needs judgment, and it is where "distributed" buys the most, because it is where A is most centralised (`SKILL.md:56-81`). `horde refine`:

- **Cut.** The architect one-shot reads the graph (`yg tree`, `yg structure`, `yg node`, `yg impact`), the charter, and Grain's map, and proposes territories: sets of whole nodes, usually a subtree, sized so that their code, rules and logs fit one context of the class assigned, with the cut level chosen per subtree ("a whole module handled by one, or only the front-end elements", his example). Territories are recorded in `.horde/hordes/<h>/territories.json` and leased across hordes in `.horde/leases.json` (the existing lease mechanism, `_lib.mjs:185-343`, rekeyed by territory). The client sees the cut in words.
- **Consult.** One one-shot per territory, in parallel, with the territory's full context and the charter, answers exactly the questions the maintainer listed: what in my territory has to change, is this a new module or a change inside one, does it break single responsibility, which pattern do I want here and is it already law, what contract do I need from a neighbour. It writes tickets (`tk new` with files, produces, consumes, evidence, class) and law proposals (an aspect it wants raised or added, with the evidence). It decides the inside; it does not decide the boundary.
- **Review.** The architect one-shot reads `queue plan --out` (kept, `queue.mjs:1199-1208`) and rules on completeness, buildability, cycles, hub files, and on every graph-change and contract proposal, exactly the five questions in `architect.md:63-80`. A hub file three tickets claim is a cut proposal, as today.
- **The frame.** The session renders it under Ratatoskr and asks only product questions. The client's go is the only approval in the whole run.

This is refinement as negotiation among contexts, which is what "self-organising on the graph" means, and it runs in one refinement pass rather than as a standing staff.

### 4.4 The territory is a context, not a seat

Adopted from B: a territory is a lease-sized set of whole nodes and the node stays Yggdrasil's, never recut to fit an agent (B's A16 is right and I would not soften it). Departure from B: a territory is the unit of *context* and *lease*, not of *approval*. There is no reviewer per territory. What a fresh territory reviewer would say about a diff is either a rule, which belongs in the law, or taste, which belongs in the log, or a design objection, which belongs to the architect's key on a graph item. B's per-territory reviewers reintroduce the fan-out it set out to cap.

### 4.5 How an owner legislates, and how the lowering guard works

**Raising needs no signature.** Any agent working a territory may, in its own branch: add an aspect under `.yggdrasil/aspects/`, attach it to a node of its territory, or run `node.mjs promote` (kept; it writes the rule's status line and records why in the rule's own log, `node.mjs:899-905,934-940`). The consult does it at refinement, the worker does it when a ticket reveals a pattern, and a **legislate** one-shot does it after a wave when a worker flagged "pattern not enforced" in a log, or when the quality index says advisory rules are clean long enough. `escalate recurring` (`escalate.mjs:279-330`) stays as the third trigger: three rulings of one kind on one territory are a rule.

**Lowering needs the client.** The landing gate runs `yg aspects --json` on the base tree and on the branch tree and refuses, unless a ruled escalation forwarded to and answered by the client names the aspect, any of: an aspect present at base and absent at head; a status lower at head than at base (Yggdrasil's own `aspect-status-downgrade` catches an attach-site downgrade below the cascade, `docs/aspect-status.md:143-144`, but not the rule's own `status:` line moving down); a changed `review_by`; a narrowed `when` or `scope` on a pre-existing aspect; a `yg-suppress` marker added (from `yg suppressions`, which needs a `--json`, section 5); an aspect detached from a node in the diff (`yg context --node --json` at base versus head). Deterministic, no model.

**Conflict of interest.** A branch that changes the *text* of a pre-existing aspect (`content.md`, `check.mjs`, `companion.mjs`, `when`, `scope`) *and* changes a code file that aspect reaches (`yg context --file --json`) cannot land without the architect's key on a graph item. Adding a new aspect or promoting one is not a conflict; sharpening a rule that refused your own code is. The worker's way out is to split: a code ticket and a legislation ticket. This is the session's "the worker whose diff fails a rule never edits that rule", made mechanical.

**What the ladder keeps.** `draft → advisory` on a clean drill corpus with cases; `advisory → enforced` after two closed waves with nothing new and no refusals (`node.mjs:503-530` reasoning, `node.mjs:859-893` the checks, `node.mjs:1105-1134` observations recorded only by a close). Unchanged, and it is the best part of 0.4.

### 4.6 How the client sees the law in their words

At `horde close`, and at `horde done`, the tool computes the law diff base→trunk from `yg aspects --json` at both trees: aspects added, statuses raised, attachments added, each with its description, the nodes it reaches (`yg impact --aspect`), and the last line of its own log (carried in `yg-aspects/1`, `docs/cli-reference.md:818-828`). It writes a machine document `horde-law/1`. The session renders it under Ratatoskr's rule (never name an artifact, say what changes for people): one sentence per rule, its reason, and the veto. "From now on, every part of the app that sends an email has to go through the one shared sender. Added because two changes each wrote their own. Say the word and it goes back." The veto is `node.mjs demote --by user --why` (kept, `node.mjs:999-1068`) or a removal as a graph item, and both leave the client's words in the rule's log. The same rendering covers the evidence catalogue: what is proven, what is not, what the client should look at themselves (a screenshot, a film), because non-text evidence is the client's final yes, not an agent's.

### 4.7 Running on ephemeral subagents alone; what teammates add

`horde tick` is a script. It reconciles (`queue.mjs:1281-1316`, kept), lands, closes, and prints a dispatch list: `{spawn: [{ticket, model, brief}], judge: [{ticket, pairs, brief}], askClient: [...]}`. It cannot spawn agents; the caller does. Without teammates, the caller is the session: it runs the tick, issues the Agent calls in one message (parallel), and when calls return it runs the tick again. The session's own tokens per tick are small (JSON in, JSON out); what it costs is the session's presence, which is the platform fact that made A need wake-ups (`SKILL.md:158-160`). With a scheduled run (`/loop` or a cron-shaped session) even that goes away. Nothing becomes impossible.

With teammates, one teammate, the **runner** (Sonnet), runs the tick loop and issues the Agent calls itself; a teammate can spawn subagents (Horde `CLAUDE.md:13`). The session then only talks to the client and answers what the tick puts in `askClient`. That is the whole difference: *where the loop lives*, exactly as Ruling 1 says. No other teammate exists; the architect and the consults are subagents of whoever runs the tick.

Liveness has no role: a worker is a call that returns. When it returns without `horde land` having recorded a sha, the next tick reconciles its branch: a commit beyond the parent is landed; a dirty tree is committed as `wip: reclaimed` and requeued with the log (`queue.mjs:1297-1305`); a clean tree is requeued. No thresholds, no roster.

## 7. How he would know it is better

He intends to test, not trust. These are the tests, each with what settles it. Researcher runs them; the field report's repository is the natural subject because its `.horde/` state and ticket logs are the record.

1. **Do the seats catch anything the gate would not?** The decisive test, and it can be run on 0.4's own records before a line of E exists. Take every `tk review … changes` and every `not-reproduced` verdict in the mission's ticket logs. Classify each finding: (a) a deterministic or judgment aspect would have refused it (write the aspect and run `yg aspect-test` or `yg drill` against the branch as it stood); (b) taste (a Minor by the review discipline's own words, `review.md:11-15`); (c) neither. The count of (c) is the value of the owner and verifier seats. If it is near zero, the seats are redundant; if it is not, each (c) is a rule the family cannot express yet, which is a Yggdrasil finding. The same split, applied to what the auditor found, measures the auditor.
2. **Cost per landed ticket.** Same repository, same charter, two runs: 0.4 and E. Measure weighted runs per landed ticket from `cost.json` (0.4 undercounts owner and steward turns, so count Agent calls and teammate turns from the transcript too), reviewer calls per landed ticket from the events file diffed per branch, wall-clock from first dispatch to trunk merge, and human turns per landed ticket (the decisions KPI 0.4 already prints, `wave.mjs:696-711`). E should show fewer runs per ticket and fewer human turns; if reviewer calls rise, the events file says by how much and whether the pairs were reached by the change.
3. **Wrong-tree incidents.** After each run: `git status --porcelain` in the trunk tree at every tick (E: empty except inside `land`), count of `wip: reclaimed` commits, count of graph files changed outside a graph item. Zero by construction in E; the run confirms the construction.
4. **Dead-by-silence.** The maximum age of a `landed` item before `land` runs on it, and the maximum age of a `queued` ready item before dispatch, read from the queue's timestamps. In E both are bounded by the tick interval; in 0.4 the field report measured twenty minutes for one ticket (H13).
5. **Coordination by prose.** Count of agent-to-agent messages (E: zero) and count of times a ruling recorded in a file was reported as absent (H25's shape).
6. **Law growth and its bite.** Per wave: aspects added or raised by territory agents; after the mission, `yg aspects --health` catch and exposure per rule, and `yg drill` on any escape recorded with `yg drill add`. A rule that never catches anything is reported as "possibly deterring", which is Yggdrasil's own honesty label; the number to watch is refusals on later tickets that would have been review comments under 0.4.
7. **Reviewer reliability, the audit as measurement.** At a configured rate, the tick re-judges a random merged ticket's prose pairs with a different tier through `yg verdict package` and compares; publish disagreements with a Wilson interval (the arithmetic from `wave.mjs:617-624` kept as a function). This replaces the auditor's refutation rate with a number about the judge rather than about a seat.
8. **Context per worker.** Bytes of brief plus files read per worker (the Agent tool reports usage) against the territory's size, for 0.4 (brief plus inlined Law plus node charter) and E (brief plus territory context). The goal in his words is context; this is its measure.
9. **Leakage into the graph.** Count of graph-text refusals per wave (item 9) and, at the end, mission words in node logs. E has no charter to leak into; the logs are the residual.
10. **The client's view.** Give the rendered `horde-law/1` to someone who has not read the code and ask them to say what each rule forbids and to veto one. If they cannot say it, the rendering fails Ratatoskr's test, not the law.

Test 1 is the one to run first, because its answer decides whether E's central move is safe, and it needs no new code.
