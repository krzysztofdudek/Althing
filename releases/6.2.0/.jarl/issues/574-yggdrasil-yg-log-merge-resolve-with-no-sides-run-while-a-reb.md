## Changelog
- Fixed, in the entry that begins "yg log merge-resolve no longer records a baseline over a rewritten log": At a rebase or cherry-pick stop it is the history of the commit the replay began on, the branch a rebase replays onto or HEAD as it stood before a cherry-pick: its log, the history its committed baseline covers (looked up in that log and in the last 50 commits of that commit's own history) and the log both sides started from. A recorded history that lies further back than those 50 commits is refused as missing, and so is one a shallow clone does not hold; there the refusal names git fetch --unshallow. At a rebase or cherry-pick stop the refusal lists the entries that are missing or changed.

Round 2: at a replay stop the guard keeps the log at the merge base of HEAD and the replayed commit, and the version HEAD's committed baseline describes, found along HEAD's first-parent line (50 commits); a committed baseline whose last entry HEAD's log never held came from another branch and is not demanded. Assumed: round 4, the rule written before the code. The target of a replay is the commit it began on: the commit a rebase replays onto, or HEAD as it stood before a cherry-pick of one commit or of several. At a stop three histories have to survive, entry for entry, in the log about to be recorded. One: the target's log as it stood when the replay began. Two: the version the target's own committed baseline describes; when the target's log still starts with it, it is at hand, otherwise it is read from the target's own history and from nowhere else. Three: the log at the merge base of HEAD and the replayed commit. Only entries that came with the replayed commit's branch may be absent: what its parent held and the target never did. The baseline the lock driver left in the working tree, or in a commit this replay made, is not consulted, so a baseline of the replayed branch cannot stand in for the target's. Out of reach: the target's log does not start with its recorded version and no commit within the walk from the target holds it. That covers the 50-commit limit, the edge of a shallow clone, and a version no commit of the target ever held. Then merge-resolve refuses and says why. In a shallow clone the step is git fetch --unshallow and the same command again; in a full clone it is to abort and settle the log on the target first, by the two-branch form or by restoring the entry.
